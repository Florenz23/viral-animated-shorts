# Script formula

How a top story-shorts channel writes its scripts, measured from 20 of its latest
transcripts with word timings. Numbers only: "n/20" means the claim holds in that many
of the 20. Run the same count on your own reference channel before you trust ours.

## The frame

| measure | median | range |
|---|---|---|
| words per short | 234 | 200–295 (19/20 within 200–246) |
| runtime | 63 s | 56–78 s (19/20 within 56–67 s) |
| speaking rate | 3.73 words/s | 3.3–4.1 |
| first word spoken at | 0.0 s | no intro, no music lead-in |
| silence after the last word | 0.3 s | it ends on the last word |
| longest pause inside a short | ~0.45 s | 19/20 never pause longer than 0.75 s |

About 230 words, read without a breath, for about 62 seconds. No greeting, channel name,
"part 2", call to action or closing question (0/20).

## 1. The hook (first sentence)

- Median **13 words** (6–20). The second sentence starts at a median **3.5 s**.
- **14/20** share one shape: *He / They* + one past-tense action verb + a concrete object +
  a place or condition. The subject is never named in the hook (0/20).
- The act is physical, drawable in one image, and looks irrational. 18/20 name a concrete
  object.
- The reason behind it is withheld until the late reveal in 17/20.
- Our own example of the shape (not from the corpus): "He painted his whole fleet like zebras."

## 2. The beats

| # | beat | position (share of words) | length | present in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook: the baffling act | 0 % | 7–20 words | 20/20 |
| 2 | Setup: who, where, what is at stake, often opened by *You see,* | 4–15 % | 15–40 | 18/20 |
| 3 | Doubt: someone mocks the protagonist | 5–35 % | 15–35 | 11/20 |
| 4 | Escalation: the plan proceeds, stakes rise; sentences open with *So / But / Then* | 20–65 % | 60–100 | 20/20 |
| 5 | Pre-reveal question (*How …? / Wait, X? Well, …*) | 55–75 % | 2–8 | 9/20 |
| 6 | Reveal: the reason behind the hook | median **73 %** (49–92 %) | median 59 | 19/20 |
| 7 | Payoff: the climax image or twist, then stop | last 5–15 % | median 18 | 20/20 |

Three orders for beats 6 and 7:
- **Climax, then explain** (8/20): show the outcome, the explanation is the ending.
- **Show, don't tell** (8/20): the climax shows the mechanism, nothing explains it.
- **Explain, then cut back** (4/20): freeze before disaster, explain, land the climax last.

## 3. Pivot words (sentence-initial)

| phrase | shorts using it | job |
|---|---|---|
| So | 17/20 | consequence, moves the plot one step |
| But | 15/20 | turn or obstacle, 2–4 per short |
| You see, | 15/20 | context drop early and/or reveal late |
| Then | 10/20 | time jump |
| And | 9/20 | stacks the climax beats at the end |
| Well, | 5/20 | answers the pre-reveal question |

## 4. Sentence craft

- Sentence length median 13 words; 12 % are 1–5 word fragments, 18 % run past 20 words.
  Mix two-word images with one or two long chained sentences.
- Reading grade median 5.4. Contractions in 20/20.
- Third person in 18/20. Hook in past tense, body in the historic present in 13/20.
- Dialogue in 17/20 (median 2 lines, 2–12 words each, no quotation marks).
- At least one comic beat in 20/20. At least one small round number in 20/20; the key number
  repeated 3–5 times in 5/20.
- Real names in 0/20: roles instead (the captain, the sheriff, the guards).
- No source or expert named (0/20). Truth is asserted in one clause (*back then*, *in real life*).

## 5. The ending

- The last sentence lands the climax and stops. 0/20 end with a question, CTA or summary.
- Naming the hook's object again in the last sentence: 9/20, a habit, not a rule.

## 6. Fill-in template (about 230 words, about 62 s)

| # | beat | words | guidance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook | 7–15 | *He/They* + past verb + concrete object. No name, no date, no greeting. |
| 2 | Setup | 15–40 | Present tense from here. Name the threat or prize. |
| 3 | Escalation | 80–120 | 2–4 steps, turning on *So* and *But*. |
| 4 | Reveal | 25–120 | Starts at 60–90 % of the words (word ~140–205 of 230). |
| 5 | Last sentence | 6–35 | Land it, then stop. |

## 7. True or made up?

> Checked across the same 20 shorts: 2 supported, 3 bent, 9 invented around a real mechanism, 1 wrong.

- The strongest form is a **fable around one true mechanism**: median 11.1M views, against
  8.3M for documented history and 3.8M for pure fiction.
- So keep the mechanism real and checkable, and let the story around it be a story.
